# D3 — Assumptions register

Each assumption has an owner, a way to validate it, and a fallback if it fails (pack p10). Rows A01–A10 close the D2 questions, which have no client answers; A11–A14 are technical assumptions behind the architecture; A15 comes from the self-set change request CR-001.

Owners are roles. In this delivery every role is held by the delivery lead.

| ID | Assumption | Source | Owner | How it is validated | Fallback if it fails | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A01 | Chest pain, breathlessness, stroke signs, heavy bleeding, loss of consciousness and suicidal intent always end triage with emergency guidance (call 112), at any hour, and open an emergency-desk ticket | Q1 | Clinical safety | Red-flag recall of 100% on w1-001 to w1-010, including night-time and mixed-language phrasings | Widen the rule list; any unmatched emergency case is added to the eval set the same day and blocks the release | Open — PR #4 |
| A02 | The assistant's database is the source of truth for the appointment book in this build; the HIS is read-only (p4) | Q2 | Delivery lead | Booking writes only to our Appointment table; FHIR mock exposes no write endpoint | Switch booking to "propose": the assistant creates a pending request the front desk confirms | Open — PR #5 |
| A03 | The front desk owns patient-facing incidents and the clinical safety role owns AI-content incidents; every hand-off is audited with an owner | Q3 | Delivery lead | Each hand-off path in W1 and W2 writes an audit row naming the owner role | Route all incidents to one review queue with a named owner field | Open — PR #5 |
| A04 | Patients type in English or Hindi-English mix; pure Hindi or Marathi script is rare at launch | Q4 | Data | 200 synthetic intakes in both styles run through specialty matching; accuracy reported per style (p10) | Add a language-detection node before the specialty match | Open — PR #4 |
| A05 | Caregiver consent defaults to 90 days and the patient can revoke it at once; revocation blocks the next read | Q5 | Compliance | Unit test: a read after expiry or revocation is refused and audited | Make expiry mandatory per grant with no default | Open — PR #6 |
| A06 | A held slot is released if the patient has not chosen within 10 minutes | Q6 | Delivery lead | Interrupt timeout test: the slot returns to free and the thread can resume with a fresh shortlist | Do not hold slots at all; re-check availability at booking and offer the next free one | Open — PR #5 |
| A07 | Most uploads are lab reports, then radiology reports, then discharge summaries | Q7 | Data | The 100 synthetic PDFs follow this mix; chunking by section is checked on a sample of each type | Chunk by fixed size with overlap where section headers are missing | Open — PR #3 |
| A08 | Reports carry name, phone, date of birth, ABHA number and a hospital MRN; Presidio needs custom recognisers for Indian phone, ABHA and MRN formats | Q8 | Compliance | Replay 200 outbound payloads through the detector and find zero identifiers (p34) | Block egress for any chunk the scrubber cannot classify | Open — PR #6 |

| A10 | A patient can ask about reports from any Sahyog site, but never another patient's | Q10 | Compliance | W2 eval includes cross-site questions for the same patient and cross-patient red-team cases | Restrict retrieval to the site of the most recent encounter | Open — PR #6 |
| A11 | A Haiku-class model reaches at least 85% specialty accuracy on the W1 set | Architecture decision 6 | Delivery lead | Run the W1 set on both candidate models and compare | Move the specialty step to a larger model; keep the small model for extraction | Open — PR #5 |
| A12 | BGE-M3 on a laptop CPU ingests a typical report within the 60-second target (E3-S1) | Architecture decision 8 | Platform | Time ingest of 10 synthetic PDFs end to end | Batch embeddings, or move to a smaller local embedding model and record the change in an ADR | Open — PR #6 |
| A13 | The whole Core build stays under the US$2.00 budget stop | Architecture decision 15 | Delivery lead | Cost meter totals from traces after every eval run | Cut eval reruns and cap development turns; never raise the stop without an ADR | Open — continuous |
| A14 | De-identified text is enough for a correct specialty match and a faithful record answer | Architecture decision 5 | Clinical safety | Compare W1 and W2 eval results with scrubbed versus raw synthetic text on 30 cases | Keep identified processing on a local model for the affected step | Open — PR #6 |
